## Usage

```tsx
import {
  VideoPreview,
  VideoPreviewFilmstrip,
  VideoPreviewPlayOverlay,
  VideoPreviewScreen,
  VideoPreviewTime,
} from "@/components/ui/motif/video-preview";

export function Take() {
  return (
    <VideoPreview src="/take-3.mp4">
      <VideoPreviewScreen>
        <VideoPreviewPlayOverlay />
      </VideoPreviewScreen>
      <VideoPreviewFilmstrip label="Seek take 3">
        <VideoPreviewTime className="absolute right-1.5 bottom-1.5 rounded-md bg-black/60 px-1.5 py-0.5 text-white" />
      </VideoPreviewFilmstrip>
    </VideoPreview>
  );
}
```

The root owns the clock and draws only the column the parts stack in. The parts read the
clock and never speak to each other, so a caller can move one, drop one, or put a part of
their own between them. There is no frame around the parts: the picture and the strip are
each already their own shape, and a card around two shapes is a third.

| Part                      | Draws                                                                         |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `VideoPreview`            | The clock, and the column the parts stack in                                  |
| `VideoPreviewScreen`      | The `<video>` in its frame; its children draw over it                         |
| `VideoPreviewPlayOverlay` | Play and pause, as a mark on the picture                                      |
| `VideoPreviewFilmstrip`   | The frames, the playhead, and the seek surface; its children draw on the axis |
| `VideoPreviewPlayButton`  | Play and pause, as an ordinary button                                         |
| `VideoPreviewTime`        | `0:02.4 / 0:06.0`                                                             |
| `useVideoPreview`         | The same state, for a part of your own                                        |

## The filmstrip is the progress bar

A generated clip is judged by what happens in it, and played it can only be read a second
at a time. The strip puts the whole length on screen at once, a frame for each slice of
it in order, so a cut, a stall or a drift in the subject is visible before anything plays.

It is also the control. Frames left of the playhead are lit and frames right of it are
dimmed, and a press or a drag anywhere on it seeks there. The picture of the clip and the
control over it are one thing, so there is no second slider to read against it.

## Where the frames come from

The strip seeks a second, unseen `<video>` to the middle of each slice and draws it into
a canvas, in order, so it fills in from the left. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is
decoded ahead of time.

The count follows the width. Each frame is a whole picture of the clip's own shape, so a
wider strip gets more frames, not wider ones. The strip is drawn into and never read back,
which is why a clip on another host needs no CORS header for its frames.

Until they arrive, and for a file that cannot be seeked, the strip is an empty bordered
track and still seeks. The frames are a reading of a control that works without them,
and a failed picture does not turn it into an error.

## The screen

A press on the picture plays and pauses. The browser's own controls are never drawn, since
the button and the strip are the controls. It loops, so a clip does not end on one still;
pass `loop={false}` to stop it on the last frame.

`className` sizes the frame the picture sits in, and its `children` are drawn over the
picture. Anything else a `<video>` takes goes straight to the video, `muted` and `poster`
among them.

## Arranging the controls

The demo above has two things and no control row: the play mark is on the picture and the
readout is on the axis. Both are parts that go where you put them, so the other layouts
are a rearrangement and not a prop.

A row of controls under the strip, for a layout that wants one:

```tsx
<VideoPreviewScreen />
<VideoPreviewFilmstrip />
<div className="flex items-center gap-3">
  <VideoPreviewPlayButton />
  <VideoPreviewTime />
</div>
```

A single row, the button beside the strip:

```tsx
<VideoPreviewScreen />
<div className="flex items-center gap-3">
  <VideoPreviewPlayButton />
  <VideoPreviewFilmstrip className="flex-1" />
</div>
```

The play overlay and the readout on the axis are literal scrims, `bg-black/50` and
`bg-black/60` over white text. What they sit on is the clip, not the theme, and no theme
colour is legible on every frame of every video.

A press on the strip seeks, and what you draw on the axis never takes the press. It is
laid over the frames with `pointer-events-none`, so the strip underneath stays the surface.

It is not muted by default. A press is the gesture the browser wants before it will play
with sound, and a clip that has sound should have it.

## Clock

`m:ss.d`, fixed. A clip is seconds long, and a clock that ticks once a second would sit
still for most of a drag. Tenths are what let the readout follow a finger. A caller who
wants frames, or a countdown, reads `currentTime` and `duration` from the hook and draws
their own part.

## Accessibility

The strip is a `role="slider"`, labelled by `label` and valued in seconds. Arrow keys move
it one second, Shift and an arrow move it five, Home and End go to either end. Nothing is
bound to Space or Enter: play and pause belong to the button and the screen, and a surface
that grabs the space bar steals it from the page.

The labels are props, not hard-coded: `playLabel` and `pauseLabel` on the button, `label`
on the strip.
